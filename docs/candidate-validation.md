# KiwiHire Coach Candidate Validation Guide

## Goal

Validate whether real New Zealand job seekers understand and would use
the complete workflow:

```text
Application + resume
        ↓
Role-specific comparison
        ↓
Resume actions
        ↓
Interview preparation
        ↓
Saved follow-up work
```

The goal is not to ask whether the idea sounds good. The goal is to
observe whether candidates can use it for a real or realistic
application and whether the output helps them decide what to do next.

## Participants

Interview 5–10 people from at least two of these groups:

- New Zealand graduate or junior technology candidates
- International candidates seeking their first local role
- Career changers translating previous experience into technology roles

Do not collect unnecessary personal information. Use sample content if
a participant does not want to share a real resume or job description.
Do not copy private resume text into this document.

## Suggested Session

Allow 25–35 minutes:

1. Introduction and consent — 3 minutes
2. Observe the participant using the workflow — 15–20 minutes
3. Follow-up questions — 8–10 minutes
4. Summarise what you heard — 2 minutes

Tell the participant:

> This is an early prototype, so I am testing the product rather than
> testing you. Please say what you are thinking. I will not store your
> resume or personal details in my research notes.

## Tasks to Observe

Do not explain the interface unless the participant is completely
blocked.

1. Register or log in.
2. Save one application and its job description.
3. Add a resume by pasting text or importing a supported file.
4. Run a resume review for the application.
5. Explain what the match score means.
6. Identify one matched, transferable, and missing skill.
7. Decide what to do with one resume recommendation.
8. Save the review.
9. Draft one interview answer.
10. Find the application again and identify the next action.

For each task record:

- Completed without help
- Completed with help
- Could not complete
- What the participant expected to happen
- Exact point of confusion, paraphrased without personal data

## Interview Questions

### Current behaviour

1. How do you currently compare your resume with a job description?
2. How do you track applications, interview stages, and follow-ups?
3. What part of preparing an application takes the most time?
4. What part makes you least confident?

### Review value

5. Which result on this page would change something you do?
6. Which result feels obvious, unclear, or untrustworthy?
7. Does the difference between matched, transferable, and missing
   skills make sense?
8. What evidence would you need before trusting the match score?
9. Did any recommendation assume experience you do not have?

### Interview preparation

10. Would you practise any of these predicted questions?
11. Is the STAR answer area useful, or would you prepare somewhere else?
12. Would the question reader and timer change how you practise?

### Product fit

13. At what point in a real application would you use this?
14. What would stop you from using it?
15. What would make you return next week?
16. Which existing tool would this replace, if any?
17. If this product disappeared tomorrow, what would you use instead?

Avoid asking only “Do you like it?” or “Would you use it?” Ask for a
recent example and observable behaviour.

## Decision Rules

- Fix a usability blocker when two or more participants cannot complete
  a core task.
- Treat a requested feature as a hypothesis until the underlying
  problem appears repeatedly.
- Do not add paid AI merely because one participant asks for automatic
  rewriting.
- Prioritise changes that improve the core resume-to-role and interview
  workflow.
- Document rejected ideas and the reason, not only accepted ideas.

## Validation Exit Criteria

The first validation round is complete when:

- 5–10 target candidates have attempted the core workflow
- At least 4 participants use realistic application content
- The top three usability blockers are documented
- Trust concerns about local matching rules are documented
- The USP hypothesis has supporting or contradicting evidence
- The next product changes are selected from evidence
